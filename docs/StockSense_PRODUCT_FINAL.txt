1. The Problem We Are Solving
There are over 12 million small retail stores in India. Almost all of them manage inventory using a notebook, gut feeling, or WhatsApp messages to suppliers. Every single day, these stores lose money because of two problems: running out of stock (stockout) and ordering too much that expires before it sells (overstock).
Picture a typical morning for Ramesh, who runs a grocery store in Vijayawada. He unlocks his store at 8am. Around 10am, a customer asks for a packet of Britannia biscuits. Ramesh looks at the shelf — empty. Sale lost. Customer trust lost.
Later that day, he finds 40 packets of local bread expiring in 2 days because he ordered too many last week. He will sell them at a loss or throw them away.
These problems happen in every small store, every day, because there is no affordable tool that tells store owners what to order, when to order it, and from whom. Large supermarkets use ERP software costing lakhs per year. That is out of reach for a kirana owner.
StockSense AI fills this gap: enterprise-level inventory intelligence, designed for the small store owner, at zero cost. It predicts problems before they happen. It recommends solutions in plain English. It sends alerts on WhatsApp — the app every store owner already uses 200 times a day.
2. What Is StockSense AI?
StockSense AI is a web-based inventory management and intelligence system. It connects to a live database, monitors stock levels automatically every day, uses artificial intelligence to predict when products will run out, scores suppliers objectively, and delivers a WhatsApp message to the store owner every morning — before problems happen — entirely without manual intervention.
It is not just a stock tracking tool. It is a proactive decision-support system. It does not just tell you what is happening — it tells you what to do next, and why.

3. Who Is This For?
Primary User: The Store Owner
The main user is the owner of a small retail store — a kirana, mini-supermarket, bakery, or convenience store. This person:
Uses a smartphone for UPI, WhatsApp, and YouTube — but is not a technology expert
Manages inventory mostly from memory and experience, not from data
Cannot afford expensive software or hire a dedicated inventory manager
Loses money regularly from stockouts and over-ordering, often without realising it
Relies on 3–8 suppliers who call on WhatsApp
Needs simple answers, not complex dashboards with 50 charts
Secondary User: The Store Helper / Manager
In slightly larger stores, a helper may use the Quick Stock Update screen each morning — entering current stock counts. This person needs a minimal interface with zero training curve: a list of products, a number field next to each, and one Save button.

Empty dashboards kill products. The onboarding checklist ensures the owner sees meaningful data within 10 minutes of signing up.

7. Keeping Stock Levels Accurate
Stock changes every day as products are sold and deliveries arrive. StockSense AI handles this through three complementary mechanisms that work together:
7.1  Automatic Daily Simulation (Hands-Free)
Every night at midnight, the system automatically estimates the day's sales. For every product that was not manually updated in the past 24 hours, it subtracts the average daily sales from the current stock. This keeps stock levels approximately accurate without the owner needing to do anything.
This is directional accuracy, not perfect accuracy. For a kirana store, knowing you have roughly 12 milk packets left (instead of exactly 11) is far more useful than stale data from three days ago showing 25. And it requires zero effort.
As real sales data accumulates in the system (via the Record Sale or Quick Update features), the system automatically recalculates average daily sales as a rolling 7-day average from actual transactions. The longer the system runs, the more accurate its estimates become.
7.2  Quick Stock Update (5 Minutes Every Morning)
For owners who want precise data, the Quick Stock Update screen is a dedicated fast-entry interface. It shows only the products that matter — those with low stock or recent movement. Next to each product is a single number input. Enter today's actual count for each, tap Save All. Done.
This is the screen the store helper opens every morning. It is intentionally simple — no navigation, no menus, just products and numbers. Designed for someone who has never used software before.
7.3  Record Delivery (When Supplier Arrives)
When a supplier delivers goods, the owner opens the Record Delivery screen. It shows all products with pending reorder suggestions for that supplier. For each delivered item, enter the quantity received and tap Confirm. The system immediately:
Increases the current stock level for each product
Updates the Last Restocked date
Changes the reorder suggestion status from Pending to Fulfilled
Resolves the active stock alert for that product if stock is now above threshold
Logs the delivery as a RESTOCK transaction in the audit trail
Updates the supplier's on-time delivery record (for future reliability score refinement)

8.2  The Reorder Threshold — Owner-Controlled, System-Suggested
A critical design principle: the reorder threshold is not a global setting and it is not hardcoded. It is set per product by the store owner, with the system providing an intelligent suggestion.
System Suggestion Formula:  Threshold = Daily Sales × Supplier Delivery Days × Safety Buffer (1.2x)

Example: Milk sells 5 units/day. Supplier delivers in 3 days. Suggested threshold = 5 × 3 × 1.2 = 18 units.

Meaning: Place a new order when Milk stock drops to 18 — enough to last until delivery arrives, plus a 20% safety buffer. Fully editable by the owner.
Owners can choose their safety buffer in Settings:
Conservative (1.5x): For perishables, unreliable suppliers, or high-demand products
Standard (1.2x): Default. Suitable for most products under typical Indian supply conditions
Aggressive (1.0x): For very reliable suppliers or when storage space is limited
8.3  WhatsApp Alert — Meeting the Owner Where They Are
Every morning at the owner's chosen time (default 8:00 AM), before the store opens, a WhatsApp message arrives on the owner's phone. It lists all products needing urgent attention with exact days remaining and a link to the dashboard.
This is the most important feature for real-world adoption. Store owners do not check software dashboards regularly. But every store owner in India checks WhatsApp before getting out of bed. Delivering intelligence on WhatsApp rather than requiring a dashboard login is the difference between software that gets used daily and software that gets forgotten after the first week.
Smart Anti-Spam Logic: If the same product was WhatsApp-alerted within the last 24 hours and its stock level has not changed, no duplicate message is sent. The owner receives one clear daily briefing, not repeated identical spam throughout the day.
9. AI Reorder Intelligence
This is the core differentiating feature of StockSense AI. Every day, after identifying all HIGH and MEDIUM risk products, the system uses Llama 3.1 (via Groq AI) to generate personalised, reasoned reorder recommendations for each at-risk product.
9.1  What the AI Analyses
Current stock level and days until the product runs out
The product's average daily sales rate (updated from real transaction data)
All available suppliers and their composite scores, delivery times, and product categories they supply
The goal of ordering approximately 30 days of supply — enough to not overstock, enough to not reorder too soon
Whether the risk is URGENT (HIGH) or PLAN AHEAD (MEDIUM)
9.2  What the AI Produces
For each at-risk product, the AI generates a recommendation card containing:
Recommended order quantity — calculated to cover 30 days of sales at the current rate
Best supplier recommendation — the highest-scoring supplier that supplies this product's category
Plain English reasoning — the AI explains why, in one clear sentence
Estimated cost — suggested quantity multiplied by unit price, shown in INR (₹)
Urgency label — URGENT (HIGH risk) or PLAN AHEAD (MEDIUM risk)
Example AI Reasoning: 'Milk 1L will run out in 2 days based on sales of 5 units/day. Ordering 150 units from Fresh Dairy Co. (Grade A, 1-day delivery, reliability 9/10) will cover the next 30 days and arrive before stockout. Estimated cost: ₹4,500.'

After approving a reorder suggestion, the system shows a pre-written WhatsApp message template addressed to the supplier with the product name, quantity, and store name already filled in. The owner taps once to open WhatsApp with this message ready to send. The order is placed in under 10 seconds.
10. Supplier Management — Objective Scoring
A silent problem in small retail: supplier relationships are managed entirely on personal familiarity. A store owner might use a mediocre supplier for years simply because they have a long relationship, without ever comparing alternatives objectively.
StockSense AI scores every supplier on three criteria and assigns a composite grade from A to D, making supplier comparison objective and data-driven.

11. Reports — Business Intelligence Over Time
StockSense AI is not just a real-time monitoring tool. The Reports page turns historical data into business insights that help the owner make better decisions over time.
Store Health Score Trend
A line chart showing the Store Health Score for the past 30 days. The owner can see: was last week better or worse than this week? Did the score drop during Diwali? Is it trending up since switching to a new supplier? This single chart tells the story of how well the store has been managed over time.
Most-Alerted Products
A ranked list of the products that triggered the most stock alerts in the past 30 days. If Milk has been HIGH risk 12 out of the last 30 days, that is a signal: either the reorder threshold is set wrong, or the supplier is unreliable, or the daily sales estimate is outdated. This insight is invisible without historical data.
Supplier Fulfillment Rate
A table showing each supplier, how many deliveries were expected vs. received, and their on-time rate. Over time, this automatically builds a real performance record for each supplier — replacing the owner's subjective memory with objective data.

13. A Complete Day With StockSense AI
This is Ramesh's day running his kirana store in Vijayawada. He has been using StockSense AI for 3 weeks.
7:45 AM — Before Opening
Ramesh wakes up and checks WhatsApp. A message from StockSense AI:
StockSense Alert — Ramesh General Stores
3 products need urgent attention:
• Milk 1L — 2 days left (10 units)
• Biscuits Pack — 1 day left (5 units)
• Tea Bags — OUT OF STOCK (0 units)
AI reorder suggestions are ready.
He taps the link. Dashboard opens. Health Score: 52 — Warning. Three red risk badges visible immediately.
8:00 AM — Quick Reorder Actions
He goes to Reorder Suggestions. Three AI recommendation cards are ready. For Milk, the card says: order 150 units from Fresh Dairy Co. (Grade A supplier, 1-day delivery). Estimated cost: ₹4,500.
He taps Approve. A pre-written WhatsApp message appears: 'Hi Rajesh bhai, I need 150 packets of Milk 1L urgently. Please confirm delivery for tomorrow morning. — Ramesh, Ramesh General Stores.' He taps Send. Done in 45 seconds.
He does the same for Biscuits. For Tea Bags (OUT OF STOCK), he calls the supplier directly from the supplier card.
9:30 AM — New Product from a Sales Rep
A new FMCG representative visits and shows Ramesh a new brand of instant coffee. Ramesh decides to stock it. He opens StockSense AI on his phone, taps Add Product, and takes a photo of the coffee jar the rep is holding. Two seconds later: 'Sunrise Premium Instant Coffee 200g — Beverages.' He enters 24 units and ₹185 price. The system suggests a reorder threshold of 8 units based on his coffee supplier's 2-day delivery. He accepts. Product added in 35 seconds.
6:00 PM — End of Day
His helper Suresh opens the Quick Stock Update screen on the store tablet. He sees 12 products listed — those with significant movement today. He updates the numbers for milk (sold 8 packets today), biscuits, and bread. Taps Save All. The system updates risk levels immediately. The dashboard's Health Score rises to 67 as tomorrow's situation looks better after the approved orders.
Next Morning — Delivery Arrives
Fresh Dairy Co. delivers 150 packets of Milk at 7:30 AM. Ramesh opens Record Delivery, selects Milk 1L, enters 150 received. The system: increases stock to 160 units, marks the Milk reorder suggestion as Fulfilled, resolves the Milk stock alert, updates last restocked date, logs the transaction. Health Score updates to 81 — Good. The red Milk badge disappears from the dashboard.
End of the Month — Review
Ramesh opens the Reports page for the first time this month. He sees his Health Score has improved from 58 to 78 over 30 days. The Most Alerted Products chart shows Biscuits has been HIGH risk 11 days out of 30 — he realises he needs to either raise its threshold or find a faster supplier. The Supplier Fulfillment table shows his bread supplier has only a 70% on-time rate — he decides to find an alternative.
These insights were invisible before StockSense AI. Now they are obvious.

16. Future Scope
Product Features
POS Integration: Connect to billing software — stock updates automatically with every sale. Eliminates estimation entirely.
WhatsApp Bot: Two-way conversation. Owner replies REORDER to approve suggestions without opening the app. Reply STATUS for current health score.
Multilingual Interface: Telugu and Hindi for non-English-speaking store owners in Andhra Pradesh and other markets.
Expiry Alerts: For perishables — flag products that will expire before they can be sold at current sales rate.
Seasonal Demand Adjustments: Automatically increase reorder thresholds for products with high festival-season demand (marked as seasonal by owner).
Mobile Native App: Offline stock updates, faster camera access, push notifications in addition to WhatsApp.
Multi-Store Management: One owner, multiple store branches, aggregated dashboard showing chain-wide health.
Business Model
Freemium: Free for stores with up to 50 products. Paid tiers at ₹299–499/month for 50–500 products.
B2B2C via FMCG Distributors: License to distributors who offer it to their retail network as a value-added service.
White Label: Branded version for regional FMCG companies to offer to their retail channel partners.
